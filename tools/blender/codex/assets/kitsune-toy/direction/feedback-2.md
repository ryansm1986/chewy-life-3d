Progress: the ears are solid now, the pouch is gone, the mouth, pupils, lashes, sleeves and tail are right. Three
things keep her from the sheet, all about proportions of big shapes.

## Locked
Round 1's list, plus the mouth line, the eye paint, the sleeves, the tail.

## Fixes
1. **The ears are too small and narrow.** On the sheet's HEAD FRONT they're big, broad triangles, almost as tall as the
   face: measure them again on the sheet with PIL. My reading: base width about **0.40 W**, height from the base's
   middle to the tip about **0.45 W**, the inner-ear triangle about 0.26 W wide at its base. Yours are about 0.31 W wide
   and look much slimmer. Keep the tip height above the crown (0.21 W) by setting the bases lower on the head's upper
   corners, with the outer edge continuing the head's outline. Also remove the pale line along the inner rim: in the
   portrait and the front it reads as a separate light-pink band between the orange rim and the inner ear. Make the rim
   orange right up to the inner-ear edge (if it's the studio highlight on a ridge, flatten the ridge).
2. **The upper head is too broad.** On the sheet the head narrows above the eyes into a rounder, smaller crown: the
   whole head is a soft pear, widest at the cheeks. Yours keeps its width up to a broad flat top, which also makes the
   head look huge from the game camera. Measure the sheet's outline above z 0.39 too; my reading is half-widths of
   about **0.38 W at z 0.50** and **0.30 W at z 0.60**, rounding onto the crown. Keep everything at and below z 0.39.
3. **The white lower face is too big.** On the model the white reaches the head's outline at about z 0.25 W on each
   side, and the lower outline flares out at the tufts, so the lower face reads as wide jowls. On the sheet the cheeks
   are orange down to about **z 0.12 W** at the outline, the white sits inside: across the muzzle, the chin and under
   the blush, with soft pointed tufts that stay inside a smooth convex outline. Lower the white's side boundary to that
   and make the tufts gentle points on a smooth curve, not a flare.
