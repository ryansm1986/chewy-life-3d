# Concept brief: oni-concept (Pawhaven enemy series, the Burrow)

The series so far: the owner approved `approved-series-example-takenoko.png` (Bamboo Grove) and
`approved-series-example-kuri.png` (Momiji Hollow). Match their style, finish and sheet layout.

The Burrow is the game's first dungeon: four little dens under the village (the Mossy Burrow, the Fox Shrine, the Oni
Kitchen, the Moon Sanctum). Its monsters are the first enemies a young player meets, so they are the simplest and
friendliest-looking of the series.

**This kind is one rigid piece** (the game bobs, squashes, tilts and spins the whole model; nothing on it moves on its
own), so design it as one solid toy figure: no separate swinging parts needed.

## The monster
**Oni Imp**, a chubby little ogre with a club, the heavy hitter of the Burrow. Packs of 1–4. About **1.35 m tall**.

What it does in a fight: it **waddles** after you and swings a big **heavy club** (a slow, hard hit).

Identity to keep (every option): a round chubby **oni** (red #ff6a5a), two little **horns** (cream), a tiger-stripe
**loincloth** (yellow and dark), a spiked **kanabō club** (toy-like, rounded studs) held in one hand, a grumpy brave
face, little fangs (toy-like).

Variant strip (same model, colourways): 1. red #ff6a5a; 2. **Blue Oni Imp**: blue #5a8aff.

**Boss callout (also draw it): Oni Chef Gorobei**, the boss of the Oni Kitchen: a big red oni (#ff5a4a, about 4.3 m
tall) in a towering white **toque** and a white **apron**, a cleaver in one hand and a ladle in the other, a moustache.
Draw a small front + 3/4 of him beside the variant strip.

## The three options (the same style, genuinely different designs)
- **Option A, "Club Imp"**: the current idea perfected: a round red imp with horns, the tiger loincloth and the club on its shoulder.
- **Option B, "Oni Kid"**: a little oni in a tiger-print happi and a headband, the club bigger than he is, a bold grin.
- **Option C, "Oni Bean"**: the most chibi: a round bean body nearly all face, tiny horns, a tiny club; the cutest.
